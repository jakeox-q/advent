# Advent Games: context from the wireframe phase

> **Read this as background, not as the spec.** This was written on 28 Oct 2026, before the visual design was done. Where it disagrees with the live design (https://advent.qagency.workers.dev/design/) or with `handoff/homepage-spec.md`, the design and the spec win. Known changes since this was written are listed at the bottom.

## 1. The project in one paragraph

Advent Games (adventgames.com.au) is a Sydney-based, owner-operated online board game and tabletop store run by Dean Verco. It turns over about $700k a year, carries around 11,000 products (mostly from supplier feeds) and is moving off Neto/Maropost onto Shopify. Q Agency is rebuilding it on Shopify using the **Enterprise** theme (Clean Canvas). The budget is low, so the build leans on Enterprise's own sections and settings. Custom work is limited to a few Custom Liquid blocks fed by metafields. The site should be simple, clear and built to convert, without feeling like a discount store with sales everywhere.

## 4. Scope and constraints

- **Up to 15 templates plus checkout.**
- **Native first.** Use Enterprise sections and blocks wherever possible, with CSS or Liquid tweaks. Custom sections only where the design says so, and they must be fully editable through section settings and blocks.
- **Excluded:** custom sections outside the theme's design, checkout customisation, complex delivery logic (bookable pickup times), copywriting (Dean supplies copy), new photography or video, app subscriptions.
- **Mobile:** must match how Enterprise natively lays it out, so it creates no extra build work.
- **Optional / not in scope:** wishlist (app), product reviews (app), "Games in this article" row on blog posts, card-sleeve matching.

## 5. Design direction (from Dean)

What he wants:
- A clear layout and good filtering. He says that's what wins.
- Snappy pages: fast to load, with instant filter changes.
- Easy browsing across a large catalogue.
- Personal service showing: customers "hear back from Dean, not an AI response".

What he dislikes:
- **Moving banners and carousels that auto-rotate.**
- **Fade-ins on scroll, fading pages and images, loading pauses.**
- **Fades when clicking filters** rather than instant updates.
- **Messy product tiles.**
- Side-scrolling sections that glitch or repeat.

What this means for the build:
- Turn theme animations off. No scroll-reveal, no auto-rotate.
- Product tiles tidy and equal height, buttons lined up along the bottom.
- Sale messaging sparingly: one seasonal campaign banner.
- Use wide screens: page width 1600px (Dean is on 2560 x 1440).

## 6. Data available

Design and build only what has a data source. Key fields:

- **Native:** title, description, price, compare-at price, barcode, images, video media, vendor (publisher), product type.
- **BGG data** (`bgg.*`): year, rating, number of ratings, rank, designers, artists, categories, mechanics, families, min/max players, min/max play time, min age, weight (displayed as "Complexity"), game type, is expansion, base game, awards.
- **Advent fields** (`advent.*`): RRP, availability state and supplier ETA, release date, supplier lead time, publisher notice, staff note, arrived date (drives "Just arrived"), "made for" game, sleeve size, pack quantity, finish.
- **RPG fields** (`rpg.*`): system (record with name, publisher, genre, image) and product kind (shown as "Category").

Anything optional must look right when empty: the block simply disappears.

**Don't add products to the dev store.** A full product import is coming from Dean and a demo product set already exists. Gaps in products, collections and menus are fine in a draft build. Point sections at whatever exists and note what's missing.

## 7. Words and copy rules

- **Complexity**, never "Weight". BGG's decimal out of 5 (e.g. 2.88 / 5).
- RPG product kind is **Category**.
- Supplier dispatch is **2-7 business days** everywhere.
- **Staff recommendations**, not "Dean recommends".
- **Preorder** = preorder with no supplier date. **Coming soon** = preorder with a supplier date. Both can be bought.
- Australian English. No em dashes. Plain, friendly and direct.
